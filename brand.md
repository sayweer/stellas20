# Brand — Everspan

Everspan separates yield-bearing assets into tradable principal and yield positions on Stellar.

_Generated by `brand-design` on 2026-08-01._

## Palette — Ember

**Vibe:** warm · serious · crafted · enduring  
**Category:** DeFi  
**Mood:** ember + parchment

The identity is a fire read against paper: a gradient that runs from the deepest
burgundy through the brand red and the ember orange out to cream, laid over a
parchment surface. There is no neutral grey anywhere in the system — every step
of the ramp sits on the line between the cream and the ember-black, because a
true grey placed beside these reads as a second palette showing through.

### Status roles

Blue-era Everspan collapsed success, caution and failure onto the brand, because
spending a second hue on meaning would have broken a single-colour identity.
An ember brand inverts that argument: a failure painted in the brand red cannot
be told apart from a primary button. So:

- **Success** takes the olive, **caution** takes the terracotta.
- **Failure** keeps the ember — the one place where red is also the meaning.
- Meaning is still carried by icons and explicit copy as well as hue, never by
  hue alone.

`src/lib/theme.test.ts` holds this: it asserts each role stays readable on its
own surface *and* that success and caution never equal the accent again.

### Semantic tokens

The Vite + Tailwind project maps these roles through `tailwind.config.js` and `src/index.css`.

**Light surface:**

```css

--foreground: #2a0e0e;
```

**Dark surface:**

```css

--foreground: #eed9b9;
--primary: #9b0f06;

```

### Surface roles

Three tokens sit beside the neutral ramp, each owning one job so no ramp step
has to serve two surfaces at once:

| Token      | Job                                                       |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| `boundary` | Edge of an interactive control. Carries 3:1 non-text contrast. |
| `hairline` | Decorative edge — card outlines and dividers. Matches ink/10. |
| `raised`   | Fill of a control that is selected, hovered, or a skeleton. |

### Radii

Three steps, no others. `rounded-full` for anything you press — text buttons,
segmented controls, chips, icon buttons. `rounded-xl` for anything holding
content — inputs, rows, nested boxes, skeletons. `rounded-2xl` for a panel.

### Contrast check

Measured, not estimated — and enforced by `src/lib/theme.test.ts`.

| Pair                              | Ratio                                  |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| night / parchment                 | 15.5:1 ✓                               |
| muted ink / parchment             | 6.4:1 ✓                                |
| parchment / primary ember         | 7.3:1 ✓                                |
| cream / ember canvas              | 14.5:1 ✓                               |
| `accent-300` / ember canvas       | 6.4:1 ✓                                |
| `positive-300` / ember panel      | 8.7:1 ✓                                |
| `warning-300` / ember panel       | 7.0:1 ✓                                |
| ember ring `#D53E0F` / canvas     | 4.3:1 — large text and graphics only   |

Use parchment text on ember fills; never night on the primary ember. On the dark
canvas, use the ember for large graphics and `accent-300` (`#DE7349`) for small
text and focus indicators — the ring tone `#D53E0F` is a shade too dark to carry
body copy there.

## Typography — Space Grotesk

- **Display + body:** Space Grotesk Variable
- **Mono:** system monospace stack

Headlines are large, compact and restrained. Numbers use `font-mono tabular-nums`.

## Gradients

The brand *is* a gradient, but it is never painted as one. `#5E0006 → #9B0F06 →
#D53E0F → #EED9B9` is sampled into the accent ramp and spent as flat fields, so
the warmth is carried by which step a surface picks rather than by a wash across
it. A literal gradient fill would soften the edges the layout depends on.

## Tone and voice

Use direct, factual language. Describe the position, rate and maturity before describing the technology. Prefer short sentences and concrete numbers.

Avoid hype, urgency, exclamation marks and claims such as “revolutionary”, “effortless” or “risk-free”. Never imply guaranteed protocol safety or guaranteed yield.

> One deposit. Two positions. Choose the rate exposure you want to hold.

## Usage dos and don'ts

**Do:**

- Use parchment `#F6EDDD` as the default marketing surface, ember-night `#2A0E0E` for high-contrast chapters, `#1B0506` panels above the `#140304` app canvas, and `#9B0F06` for primary actions and brand moments.
- Keep one ember action per viewport whenever possible.
- Use a 4 px spacing grid and large, intentional whitespace.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` and keep all content usable without animation.

**Don't:**

- Hardcode brand colors in component files; use Tailwind tokens or CSS variables.
- Use the brand ember for market *direction*. Ember means Everspan and, in the one case of a failed transaction, failure — it never means "up" or "down". A rate below zero is marked with a sign glyph, not a colour.
- Reach for olive or terracotta as decoration. They are the success and caution tones, and spending them elsewhere makes a status card mean nothing.
- Add decorative gradients, glassmorphism or excessive shadows.
- Copy Paribu assets, text or exact layouts; translate the visual principles into Everspan’s product story.

The ramp runs along the line from the cream seed to the ember-black one, so there is no neutral grey in the system at all. On the light surface it inverts: the canvas becomes parchment and cards separate by moving toward white rather than away from it.
